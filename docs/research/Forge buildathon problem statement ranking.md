# Pick T2.1 as a proactive voice agent

**Recommendation: choose Theme 2, Problem Statement 1 (T2.1, Multilingual Agritech and Resource Advisory Assistant), built as a proactive outbound voice agent instead of an inbound chatbot.** In plain language: the team builds "a farmer's assistant that phones you before the rain or the price move." A farmer gives a crop, sowing date and village (the demo uses real consenting people). Every few hours an agent pulls the live forecast, local soil moisture, nearby mandi prices and any government weather alert. If something matters to that crop at that stage (rain on a crop near harvest, a price jump, a lightning alert), it rings the farmer in their language, speaks crop-specific protective advice and a revised sowing/harvest schedule, takes a spoken reply ("harvest earlier?"), and updates the plan. This scores about **80/100** against the Forge rubric, against 77 for the runner-up (an alert-to-action dispatch agent built on T2.4) and 66 to 72 for most others. The score is judgement, not measurement. The recommendation also depends on a data test that has not yet passed: the mandi-price API needs a free key and was never tested with one, and no source for per-farm soil data was verified. The event dates (29 Oct to 1 Nov 2026) and the registration deadline (30 Oct 7:30 PM IST) come only from a third-party copy of the Unstop listing, so the team must confirm that registration is still open before spending any time ([GitHub monitor copy of the listing](https://github.com/Pranavdeshmukhhh/HackRadar-AI/issues/40)). The notes found no public evaluated deployment that places proactive, personalised, LLM-driven outbound calls on live forecast and mandi triggers. That is a gap in what was searched, not proof that nobody does it.

## Top five, each with the build

### 1. M1: T2.1 as a proactive voice advisor (score 80)

**Target user.** A smallholder farmer with a basic phone, in one or two districts, growing one or two crops. The framing is that the farmer does not have to ask. The crop-damage context is real but its statistics conflict: one report counts about 9.47 million hectares of crop damage from extreme weather in Jan to Sep 2025, with a later page giving 17.41 million hectares for the full year, and a farmer survey headline of 80% of small farmers losing crops over five years is contradicted by a different outlet's figure of about 50% ([CSE](https://www.cseindia.org/india-experienced-extreme-weather-events-on-99-per-cent-of-the-days-in-the-first-nine-months-of-2025-says-cse-and-down-to-earth-s-climate-india-2025-report-an-annual-assessment-of-extreme-weather-events-12940); [NewsArena](https://www.newsarenaindia.com/nation/80-of-india-s-marginal-farmers-suffer-crop-losses/15119)). Use these only as "reported", never as settled fact.

**Solution (covers every T2.1 requirement).** The farmer registers by a short call or web form with crop, sowing date, village and language. The system delivers: conversational and voice delivery (outbound call plus the farmer can talk back, with a typed web fallback); weather forecasts (Open-Meteo, 48-hour and 7-day); mandi prices for the farmer's crop in nearby markets (data.gov.in); local soil metrics (see sources); crop-level protective advice (retrieval over real ICAR and state agromet advisory documents, always citing the source passage); and planting and harvesting schedules (computed from the farmer's sowing date plus crop-duration guidance extracted from the same ingested documents, then shifted by forecast, for example "harvest by Thursday, rain Friday"). Languages: start with Hindi and one regional language the team can verify by ear, because Bolna lists 10+ Indian languages including Hinglish, Hindi, Tamil and Telugu ([Bolna pricing](https://www.bolna.ai/pricing)).

**Agentic loop.** A scheduler wakes every few hours. For each farmer it fetches forecast, soil and mandi data and reads SACHET alerts. A decision step (rules for thresholds, LLM for reasoning about crop stage) checks triggers, for instance rain over a threshold within 48 hours on a crop near harvest, or a mandi price above its recent average. It retrieves the relevant advisory passages, composes a short spoken message in the farmer's language, places the call, listens for the reply ("harvest earlier?"), writes the decision and a new schedule back to the farmer's profile, and re-checks next cycle. Dedupe keys stop repeat calls, and an unanswered call falls back to a text message.

**Live data sources and status.**

| Source | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Open-Meteo forecast | Weather, 7-day forecast | **Tested** (no key). Free tier is non-commercial, 10,000 calls/day documented ([pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing)). |
| NDMA SACHET RSS | Government weather alerts (IMD, CWC, state) | **Tested.** Titles in multiple languages, empty descriptions; district geography **unverified** ([feed](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/cap_public_website/rss/rss_india.xml)). |
| NASA POWER | Daily historical agro-weather | **Tested** ([POWER](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/daily/point)). |
| data.gov.in mandi prices | Price trigger | **Unverified.** Free key needed; page said "request" on one result; real resource ID and freshness not confirmed ([dataset](https://data.gov.in/resource/current-daily-price-various-commodities-various-markets-mandi)). Reported as sparse and sometimes down ([Agmarknet MCP note](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/Krishna-Baldwa/agmarket-mcp)). |
| Soil metrics | Local soil moisture and properties | **Unverified.** Open-Meteo soil moisture/temperature variables and NASA POWER soil wetness are possible sources but were not tested in the notes; SoilGrids returned 504 once. No verified per-farm soil API in India was found. |
| ICAR/state advisories and crop calendars | RAG corpus | **Unverified.** Documents not yet located or fetched. |
| Bolna, Sarvam | Voice agent, STT/TTS | **Documented** (Bolna from about 6 cents/min plus telephony; Sarvam TTS Rs 3 per 1,000 characters; [Sarvam pricing](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)). Not tested for India outbound calls. |

**Demo script (about 2.5 minutes).** (1) 15 seconds: the problem in one sentence and the screen showing live fetch timestamps. (2) 20 seconds: onboard a real teammate-farmer by phone, with crop and sowing date. (3) 40 seconds: dashboard shows the live forecast, today's mandi price, soil reading and a SACHET alert, each with its source and fetch time. (4) 60 seconds: the agent decides, and the teammate's phone rings on stage; the call is spoken in Hindi, gives protective advice with a source, and the teammate answers "harvest earlier". (5) 15 seconds: the schedule on screen updates and the decision log shows why.

**USP.** Proactive, crop-stage-personalised, closed-loop voice, with each statement traced to a source passage. The notes found this only in vendor pitches and practitioner write-ups, not in evaluated public deployments, so say "not found in the public evaluated deployments the research covered" ([ADB deck](https://airdrive.eventsair.com/eventsairseasiaprod/production-adb-public/0ef7f1661c43456b9f5e03309470dce9); [Ikontel](https://www.ikontel.com/industries/agriculture-Industry-communication-solutions)).

**Main risks and fallbacks.** Telephony and Indian regulation: outbound commercial calls need regulated numbers and DLT registration, and Twilio trial accounts can only dial verified numbers with a 10-minute cap ([Twilio India](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/in/voice)); fallback is a consented call to team phones through Bolna, with an in-browser voice widget behind it. Mandi data failure: pivot the trigger to weather and SACHET and show mandi as "unavailable at last fetch", never a made-up price. Hallucinated agronomy: answer only from retrieved passages, refuse otherwise. Fake-profile risk: use real consenting people and say so; ideally one real farmer or relative joins. Stretch goal only if time remains: when a damaging event hits, the agent walks the farmer through the PMFBY 72-hour loss intimation (helpline 14447), which borrows A4's strongest hook.

### 2. M2: T2.4 as alert-to-action dispatch (score 77)

**Target user.** A district relief coordinator, or outdoor and gig workers, in a district with an active alert. **Solution.** The agent watches SACHET and weather, finds alerts that intersect an active route or worker location, reroutes (OSRM, tested), drafts a dispatch or "work or wait" message, and sends it for human approval. Teammates' phones (Traccar or OwnTracks) act as a genuinely live fleet; the supplier or inventory side has no public API in India, so any inventory layer is a disclosed pilot partner, not a mock. **Agentic loop:** poll feeds, detect a new alert near a route, recompute, notify, draft order, wait for approval, log. **Sources:** SACHET and OSRM tested; Open-Meteo tested; CWC flood data and GDACS unverified; openrouteservice avoid-polygons unverified. **Demo:** show the live feed, then a rerouting on a teammate's phone. **USP:** an end-to-end loop from official alert to action. **Risks:** nothing relevant may fire during the demo. **Fallback:** replay a real past CAP alert from the feed history, labelled as a replay, which is exactly the open question to put to organisers. A heat angle is the wrong season, since the notes' strongest heat evidence (48,156 suspected heatstroke cases in 2024) is for summer, so build for lightning, flood and rain ([Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/indias-patchy-heatwave-preparedness-sparks-fears-of-rising-death-toll)).

### 3. A4: PMFBY crop-loss claim agent (score 72)

**Target user:** a farmer whose crop was damaged by hail, flood or cyclone. **Solution:** on a damage event, the agent speaks the 72-hour rule, collects what is needed for intimation, produces a dated claim record and a tracker for status checks. **Loop:** detect event, guide intimation, track status by receipt number, escalate to the appeal route. **Sources:** SACHET and Open-Meteo for the event trigger (tested); claim status on pmfby.gov.in needs a user's own receipt number (unverified for unauthenticated use). **Demo:** a real volunteer's case or a documented public record. **USP:** the claim-and-appeal layer, not another advisory bot; a government WhatsApp bot (7065514447) already exists, so position around deadlines and evidence ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/10/01/nrg8-rj-crops.html)). **Risks:** settlement-timeline figures conflict (15 days versus 30 to 45 days across blogs), and a no-mock demo is hard. **Fallback:** fold it into M1 as the stretch described above.

### 4. T1.2 grievance triage with closure checking (score 70)

**Target user:** residents of one city and one issue class such as potholes. **Solution:** a photo or voice note is classified for type and severity by a vision model, geotagged, deduplicated by geohash and embedding, routed to the right authority, drafted as a formal complaint, and later checked when a "resolved" status is claimed by asking nearby citizens for a second photo. **Evidence for need:** Bengaluru residents report complaints closed without action, and an analysis found 61% of complaints on roads under 12 m wide, though the date is unclear ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2Fpothole-complaints-on-bengaluru-local-roads-ignored-finds-study-1238071.html)). **Sources:** no live public complaint API found; backtest only on the IChangeMyCity archive, marked deprecated on AWS ([AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/ichangemycity/)); ward boundaries unchecked. **Demo:** teammates submit photos live. **USP:** independent accountability and closure verification; severity has no ground truth, so say so. **Risk:** a judge may not count the app's own database as live official data. **Fallback:** typed descriptions instead of photos, and a pre-uploaded real photo clearly labelled.

### 5. T1.4 claim-matching fact-check with provenance (score 68, tied with T1.1)

**Target user:** a WhatsApp-style forwarder checking a viral claim. **Solution:** extract the claim, search the Google Fact Check Tools API and recent fact-check feeds, return a sourced verdict if matched, otherwise run provenance (reverse search, keyframes, metadata) and report "unverified" honestly, with a regional-language voice reply. The key insight from the MCA tipline report, that only about 2% of reviewed media were actual deepfakes and 55% of submissions were spam, argues for claim matching over a detector ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/09/223-deepfake-analysis-unit-report-most-media-alterations-done-with-basic-tools-only-2-deepfakes/)). **Sources:** Google Fact Check API needs a key and is labelled v1alpha1 (unverified, quota unknown); PIB RSS returned 403 from the test fetcher; X and Meta feeds are closed. **Risks:** detector false positives, defamation if a verdict lacks a citation. **Fallback:** show the matched fact-check link and confidence band only. T1.1 (notification-to-voice) ties on score but faces Jugalbandi and Bhashini incumbents, and Bhashini needs registered keys; production use needs a paid-tier contact ([Bhashini docs](https://bhashini.gitbook.io/bhashini-apis/)).

## Two-day build plan and the go/no-go test

The listing shows 29 Oct 4:35 PM IST to 1 Nov 6:00 PM IST, so roughly two full days remain from the evening of 30 Oct, if the listing is right. It also shows registration closing 30 Oct 7:30 PM IST, and the data tests in the notes ran at about 20:15 IST, so confirm registration status immediately. **Go/no-go test (first 60 to 90 minutes, before any build).** Run six checks and record the result with timestamps. First, get a data.gov.in key and confirm the mandi resource ID returns records dated within 3 days for one state. Second, call Open-Meteo for soil moisture and soil temperature variables at one village and confirm numbers return. Third, confirm a fallback soil source (NASA POWER soil wetness or SoilGrids) returns a value twice in a row. Fourth, place one Bolna outbound call to a consenting Indian phone from a fresh agent and listen for Hindi quality and latency. Fifth, locate and download at least three ICAR or state advisory documents for the two target crops. Sixth, check whether SACHET items carry district geography. Decision rule: go with M1 if checks 2, 4 and 5 pass and either check 1 or 3 passes. If the mandi key fails, M1 still works with weather, soil and SACHET triggers, but the T2.1 mandi requirement is then unmet and must be disclosed. If the call fails, switch to M2 (needs no key) or run M1 with a browser voice widget. If the registration or eligibility check fails, nothing else matters.

**Build plan.** Hours 0 to 2: go/no-go test, repo created, architecture diagram drafted (commit timestamps matter for the pre-existing-project rule). Hours 2 to 12: data fetchers with timestamped logging, farmer profile onboarding, RAG over fetched advisories. Hours 12 to 24: trigger logic, message composer, Bolna agent and call webhook, reply handling. Hours 24 to 36: web dashboard showing every live fetch, deployment on an always-on host (Render free tier sleeps after 15 minutes without traffic, so use a paid instance or external scheduler), retries and fallbacks ([Render docs](https://render.com/docs/free)). Hours 36 to 44: freeze code, record the 2 to 3 minute video twice, write README and 5 to 7 slides. Final hours: three full rehearsals, one backup laptop, and a recorded call as a disclosed fallback. Vihan owns the voice agent, conversation script, demo and pitch; a coder teammate owns fetchers and deployment; every member must be able to explain every component, so use a plain Python scheduler loop rather than a framework. ## What to confirm with organisers

AI tools are allowed, so that is not an open question. The open questions, most important first, are these. **Timestamped cache of real fetched data:** whether a cache that always tries a live fetch first and shows the fetch time, source URL and a "last live fetch" indicator counts as live data rather than static data; the notes found no Forge text on this. **Replay of real past alerts:** whether replaying an actual historical SACHET alert, clearly labelled, is acceptable when no relevant alert fires during the demo. **Real consenting users as "farmers":** whether profiles created by teammates or volunteers who give real crop details count as live data, or as fake placeholders. **Reference documents:** whether ICAR and state advisory documents and crop-duration guidance, ingested as a retrieval corpus, count as an official open dataset. **Registration and eligibility:** whether registration is still open after 7:30 PM IST on 30 Oct and whether the listing's eligibility text is correct. **Outbound calls:** whether a consented outbound call to team phones is acceptable for the demo, given TRAI rules and that the rules on AI telemarketing were reported as under consideration with binding status unconfirmed ([Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/trais-crackdown-on-spam-calls-and-ai-driven-telemarketing)). **Third-party platforms:** whether using a hosted voice platform with a new agent counts as building during the event. **Hardware and replicas:** only needed if the team picks a physical rig for T2.3.

## Conclusion

The decisive finding is that the rubric rewards a verified, repeatable live path more than a clever idea, and the notes show that very few of the sources named in the problem statements were verified as usable today. That favours T2.1 on a narrow condition: the data test must pass first. The recommended build is a different product from the listed statement, a phone call instead of a chat window, but it still delivers every T2.1 element, and its risk lies in specific places (the mandi key, the soil source, telephony) that can be tested in the first 90 minutes.

The honest uncertainty is larger than the 80 versus 77 gap. If the mandi key and the soil source both fail, M2 becomes the better choice because it rests on the tested SACHET feed and OSRM and needs no key. Each decision should be set by the test, not by the ranking.
