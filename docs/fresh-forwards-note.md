# What the 8 fresh forwards showed (1 Nov 2026, evening)

Rahul collected 8 real messages from family WhatsApp groups (4 scams, 4 genuine; names and numbers removed, wording
kept). No rule had been written from them, so they were the first honest test.

| | Scams rated High risk | Scams cleared as fine | Genuine messages rated High risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| The 20 older scams (the rules were written from advisories like these) | 16 of 20 rules-only | 0 | 0 of 3 |
| The 4 fresh scams, **before** any rule change | **1 of 4** (rules-only); AI run: 20 of 24 overall | 0 | 0 of 4 |
| The 4 fresh scams, after widening four wording rules | 4 of 4 | 0 | 0 of 4 |

Reading it honestly:
- On messages the rules had never seen, Parakh caught 1 of 4 as High risk and rated the other 3 "Be careful". None were
  cleared, and no genuine message was accused, but it caught far fewer than on the set it was tuned on.
- The 3 misses name nothing checkable (no link, no registration number, no firm), so only the wording rules can act.
- The four rule changes (ReKYC and "avoid account blocking", expiring reward points, "financial assistance between",
  "Yojana" with a link) are based on how these scams are described in public advisories. They fixed these four, but
  because they were written after seeing them, the "4 of 4" is a development result, not a measure of accuracy.
- The only fair number for judges is the "before" row. A new batch of unseen messages is needed to measure again.
